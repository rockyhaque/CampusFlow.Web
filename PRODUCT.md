# Product

## Register

product

## Users

CampusFlow serves a university community across four roles:

- **Admin** — university staff who oversee the platform, approve organizers, manage users, and view platform-wide analytics.
- **Organizer** — faculty and student clubs who create events, manage volunteers, sell tickets, run check-in, and send announcements.
- **Volunteer** — students who apply to events, fulfill responsibilities, earn hours, and receive certificates.
- **Attendee** — students who browse events, purchase tickets, attend, and leave feedback.

Users are typically on campus or between classes, often multitasking. They need to complete tasks quickly: find an event, buy a ticket, apply to volunteer, check someone in, or review dashboard status.

## Product Purpose

CampusFlow UVMS digitalizes the full lifecycle of university events — from organizer onboarding and event creation, through volunteer management and ticketing, to attendance tracking, certificates, and post-event feedback.

Success looks like: organizers run events without spreadsheets; volunteers and attendees move through flows with minimal friction; admins have clear oversight; and the interface feels trustworthy enough for institutional use.

## Brand Personality

**Professional · Warm · Efficient**

The UI should feel like a capable campus tool, not a flashy startup landing page. Warmth comes through clear language, approachable spacing, and helpful states — not decorative gimmicks. Efficiency means role-relevant information first, with fast paths through high-frequency workflows (events, tickets, attendance, dashboards).

## Anti-references

- Do not replace the established university brand colors — preserve the existing purple accent palette and institutional identity.
- Avoid generic gray SaaS dashboards, stock admin templates, and bland card-on-card layouts.
- Avoid over-designed marketing aesthetics: heavy gradients, scroll gimmicks, or loud hero sections inside app workflows.
- Avoid cluttered admin panels that dump too much on screen at once.
- Avoid dark, gaming-style, or overly playful UIs that undermine institutional trust.

## Design Principles

1. **Role-first clarity** — Each surface should answer "what can I do here?" within seconds. Dashboards, nav, and CTAs align to the user's role and current task.
2. **Preserve institutional identity** — Refine and professionalize the existing visual system; do not rebrand away from the university palette.
3. **Workflow efficiency** — Optimize for the paths users repeat: browse events, manage an event, buy a ticket, apply to volunteer, check in, leave feedback.
4. **Trust through craft** — Tables, forms, empty states, and error handling should feel deliberate and reliable — the UI earns confidence through consistency, not decoration.
5. **Consistent system, many surfaces** — Auth, dashboards, events, tickets, feedback, and profile should feel like one product, not separate templates stitched together.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard best practices: sufficient color contrast, visible focus states, semantic structure, and meaningful labels on interactive elements. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for animations. No formal WCAG certification target beyond sensible defaults.
